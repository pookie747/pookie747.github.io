For grown-ups

The quiet idea behind the play.

Little Clearing was built on a simple belief: the skills that help children feel steadier in the world shouldn't only live in a therapist's office. They can grow gently, through play, in a place that never feels like a lesson.

Why “therapy through gaming”

Plenty of kids — and honestly, plenty of adults — go quiet at the word therapy. It can feel heavy, or like something's wrong. So a child who would benefit enormously from learning to name a feeling, sit with a hard choice, or rehearse a tough conversation often never gets the chance to practise any of it.

These games take a softer road. A child thinks they're running a lemonade stand or talking their way through a tricky moment with a parent. What they're actually doing is building emotional regulation, decision-making, and self-confidence — the same muscles a good counsellor would help them strengthen, just wrapped in something that feels like fun.

No pressure. No labels. No “now let's talk about your feelings.” Just a calm clearing where those skills can quietly take root.

What the games gently build

🍋
Money Quest

Confidence and patience with money — earning, waiting, planning, and recognising when something's too good to be true. Practical resilience, dressed up as a game.

💬
TalkQuest

Rehearsing the conversations that scare us — owning a mistake, asking for help, being honest. A safe place to practise brave words before they're needed for real.

✨
Spark Catcher

Confidence in what makes you, you — noticing what lights you up, whether that's building, art, helping, or exploring. Self-knowledge, dressed up as a game.

🦊
Feelings Forest

A wider vocabulary for big feelings — noticing what's happening inside and finding the words for it. Emotional literacy, dressed up as a walk in the woods.

🐢
Patience Pond

Sitting with wanting something now, and waiting anyway. Patience and impulse control, one calm pond at a time.

A gentle, honest note: these games are a warm supplement, not a replacement for professional care. If your child is struggling in a way that worries you, please reach out to a doctor, counsellor, or trusted mental-health professional. Little Clearing is here to help on the everyday days — not to stand in for real support when it's needed.

A safe little corner

There are no ads, no chat with strangers, and nothing to buy. Each game runs right in the browser, nothing is collected about your child, and the whole place is built to feel calm rather than overstimulating.

It's meant to be a spot you can hand your child without a second thought — and maybe even sit and play alongside them.
